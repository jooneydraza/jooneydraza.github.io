Do I need to buy an ISBN for KDP and IngramSpark?

Updated 31 October 2026 · 2 min read · Jooneyd Raza

Short answer

If one print edition will sell through both KDP and IngramSpark, yes: each platform's free ISBN works only on that platform, so an ISBN you own is the only kind both accept. Ebooks don't need an ISBN on KDP, but each print format, such as paperback and hardcover, needs its own number.

Free ISBNs and their limits

On KDP, ebooks and low-content books are the only formats where an ISBN is optional. Every free ISBN comes with limits.

  • KDP's free ISBN is for paperback and hardcover only, shows the imprint as “Independently published” and can only be used on KDP.
  • IngramSpark's free ISBN is offered to US authors only and is owned by IngramSpark. It shows the imprint as “Indy Pub”, can't be transferred or used on other platforms, and the book must be in IngramSpark's wholesale distribution.
  • Draft2Digital assigns a free ISBN too, but it can't be used outside Draft2Digital's services.

Using KDP and IngramSpark together

IngramSpark says you can publish print on both platforms as long as you don't select KDP's Expanded Distribution. It also says a KDP-owned ISBN cannot be transferred. US authors can buy their own ISBN from Bowker, or through IngramSpark at a discounted $85.

Where to buy your own ISBN

  • US: Bowker (paid).
  • UK and Ireland: Nielsen ISBN Store (paid).
  • Australia: Thorpe-Bowker (paid).
  • Canada: Library and Archives Canada, free for eligible Canadian publishers. New accounts can take up to two months.
  • New Zealand: the National Library, free for eligible registrants located in New Zealand.
  • Other countries: the International ISBN Agency.
